The point of view

Stop blaming employees for problems built into the workflow.

An employee is expected to find a rule buried in five tabs, interpret a policy nobody clarified, and keep a customer waiting, then gets graded on the outcome. Another training session will not repair a missing decision.

Observe real frontline work, identify unnecessary memory and search burden, clarify which decisions belong to the organization, and design support around the moment of action. AI is an option, not the starting point.

Founder

Erica Love

Founder, Agent First · Principal, Agent First Consulting

Frontline operations · decision systems · human-centered AI

“Why are people still being asked to remember things software already knows?”

That question became the beginning of Agent First. I became interested in the enormous amount of human work that is really searching, remembering, switching systems, reconstructing context, interpreting rules, and carrying procedural complexity in working memory.

My work starts there. Not with the newest model. Not with a feature list. With the person trying to do the job, and the question of what the surrounding system should be responsible for instead.

The result is part operating-model design, part product design, part knowledge architecture, and part applied AI. The goal is simple: make difficult work easier to do correctly without designing the humanity out of it.

Start with the problem

Are your frontline employees doing the system’s job?

Good. That's usually where the interesting work starts.

Tell me who does the work, what they must remember or find, which decisions slow them down, and what happens when the answer is unclear. You do not need a technical specification.
